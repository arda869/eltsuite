# Lesson content standard — 1 November 2026

This is an editorial standard for original learning materials, not a visual rebrand or model fine-tune. The supplied elementary and higher-level coursebook examples inform principles; do not reproduce their content.

## Acceptance criteria
1. **Startable:** learners can tell what to use, what to do and how to respond. Instructions are short and level-appropriate. Teacher procedures stay in notes.
2. **Calibrated:** language, cognitive load, literacy, age and response demands fit the class. Low English proficiency does not imply low intelligence.
3. **Connected:** tasks build towards the intended outcome; useful language recurs naturally. No compulsory PPP, listening-first or grammar sequence.
4. **Complete:** texts, prompts, role cards, examples and keys exist. Required images/audio are available or an honest, usable alternative is identified. Media generation remains separate implementation work.
5. **Discoverable:** an inductive task requires thought before revealing its explanation. Direct teaching remains legitimate where selected deliberately.
6. **Accurate:** keys are defensible, alternatives are accepted where appropriate, examples are unscored and numbering agrees.
7. **Purposeful:** visuals and pronunciation serve a learning action. Format variety is justified.
8. **Meaningfully supported:** optional versions change support within the same objective; they are complete activities, not extra work or fixed learner labels.
9. **Evidence-aware:** prior topics show exposure, not mastery. Missing answers do not prove inability.
10. **Usable independently when required:** homework/practice must not depend on a teacher reading an unavailable script. Exams preserve their assessed construct.

## Level calibration
- A1–A2: concrete situations, manageable chunks, demonstrations and supported rehearsal leading to a small independent exchange. Adult-relevant content for adults.
- B1–B2: connected input, noticing and reuse; reasons, clarification and negotiation where useful. Avoid excessive constraints.
- C1–C2: nuance, stance, register and discourse. Challenge through interpretation and precision rather than verbosity.
- Young learners: also adapt literacy, attention and task mechanics.

## Editorial examples
- Replace “Demonstrate pragmatic competence through collaborative negotiation” with “Choose a place together. Give a reason.”
- Replace a generic “Extra task” with a complete optional version using a mixed word bank, sentence frame or planning prompts where a barrier warrants it.
- For spoken input, give an initial listening purpose and a different later task. Reveal the script for later noticing or a deliberate access route.
- Keep “Monitor for polite refusals” in teacher notes. Give learners the actual situation and response task.

## Evaluation set
Review the finished output for these briefs, rather than its plan alone:
- Adult A1 introductions: short everyday exchanges, clear example, names/places as fictional options, relevant rhythm practice, simple independent greeting.
- Adult A2 town vocabulary: labelled visual matching only if images exist, retrieval with labels hidden, short contextual use.
- Adult B1 weekend suggestions: listening purpose, meaningful noticing, controlled rehearsal and a feasible shared decision; private role information remains separate.
- Adult C1 interview responses: inference, register and evaluation followed by original responses; avoid merely longer elementary exercises.
- Independent B1 listening homework without an audio asset: must identify the missing recording and provide an honest alternative, never claim a player exists.
- Mixed-evidence class: support recommendations cite available evidence and leave unobserved performance uncertain.

For each output record: brief, model/version, generated learner material, defects by task reference, teacher corrections and acceptance. A successful schema or browser test is not evidence of teaching quality. Teacher approval of a complete reference lesson is the next qualitative checkpoint.

## Young learners
Select **Young learners** in the class profile. Put ages, literacy/readiness, available resources and relevant access needs in the existing learning-context field. These details inform generation and the plan review; adult A1 classes do not receive the young-learner instructions.

Evaluate an early-primary pre-reader food lesson and an older-primary reading-ready story lesson separately. Expect short actionable instructions; a small reused language set; oral recognition and modelling before independent production where useful; purposeful repetition; achievable turns and transitions; complete original stories/chants where selected; and observable formative checks. Do not assume that pictures, singing audio or independent reading exist. Alternative access must preserve the aim.

This work does not modify visual identity, generate image assets or add automatic audio production. Those capabilities must supply real assets before asset-dependent tasks can be considered ready.
