Professional learning

Keep your questions about teaching alive

A useful question can come from a lesson, a conversation or something you read. Keep the evidence and the thinking together, then decide what you want to explore next.

Reading

Keep the source beside the idea

Save a book or article reference, add your notes and keep useful quotations with their page numbers. Use citations in your writing and keep references organised as your project develops.

It can be a place for course reading, preparation for an assignment or your own professional interests.

Language

Investigate a question properly

Look at language examples in context. Compare what you notice, annotate evidence and build a language investigation. Where enabled, AI analysis offers another perspective for you to review.

Example sentences can illustrate usage. They do not establish how frequent a word or phrase is, or represent every variety of English.

Reflection

Think it through with Teacher's Companion

Bring a teaching question to Teacher's Companion, where it is enabled. Use the conversation to explore possibilities, reflect on a lesson or consider a small change to try.

Its suggestions draw on the context available to that conversation and your settings. You choose what to keep and what to put into practice.

Teacher training

Make room for practice and discussion

Where teacher-training access is enabled, organise sessions and use structured planning and language-analysis activities. Bring reading, shared documents and teaching questions into the conversation.

Microteaching rehearsal can help a trainee practise an explanation and reflect on it before returning to their plan.

Take one useful idea back to your teaching

Keep the note, ask the question and try something small. Professional learning becomes useful when you can connect it with the work in front of you.
